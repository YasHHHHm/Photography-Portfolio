Most photographs of the Valley of Flowers are made in July, under a blue sky, with the meadows at full colour. I went in September, when the season is turning and the monsoon has not quite let go. The sky never lifted once. I would go back in exactly the same week.

## The walk in

The valley sits high in Uttarakhand's Chamoli district. It is a national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and no road goes into it. The road ends near Govindghat; from there you walk up to Ghangaria, the last village, and the valley is a few hours' climb above that. Nobody sleeps inside the park. Every evening you walk back down to Ghangaria, so the valley only ever exists for you in daylight.

![Rows of white and green tents on a grassy slope below a forested mountain wrapped in cloud](valley-of-flowers-2026/camp-01.avif "wide")

The camp sat under a forested wall whose top we never saw. Cloud came down to the treeline every afternoon and stayed there. I spent the first evening learning the light: flat, soft and very bright inside the cloud, so the camera wants to darken everything. I exposed for the green and let the sky go white. Here, the white is the sky.

## Water out of the cloud

The trail follows water the whole way. Streams cross it, and waterfalls hang off the valley walls with their tops lost in the cloud, so they seem to start in mid-air.

![A thin waterfall threads down a steep green mountainside, its top lost in thick cloud](valley-of-flowers-2026/waterfall-01.avif)

I framed this one vertically, to keep as much of the fall as the cloud allowed and give the green slopes room on either side. A thread of white this thin disappears in a wide frame. Closer in, it becomes the whole picture.

Further along, the river has cut beneath rock walls that lean out over the water. Standing under one is the quickest way to understand the scale of this place.

![A hiker with a backpack stands on a boulder beneath a towering, overhanging rock wall](valley-of-flowers-2026/rock-wall-01.avif)

That is me on the boulder: partly for scale, and partly because I could not stop looking up.

## Into the valley

Then the walls open out, and the valley does what its name promises.

![A meadow of small white flowers leading into a valley between dark cliffs, cloud filling the gap](valley-of-flowers-2026/valley-01.avif "wide")

By September the bright pinks and blues of high summer have mostly gone, and the meadows turn white: thousands of small flower heads, all leaning the same way. Under a grey sky they glow a little, almost like frost. I kept the flowers large across the bottom of the frame and let the cliffs and the cloud carry the top, so the eye walks in the way I did.

The British mountaineer Frank Smythe wandered in here in 1931, on his way back from climbing Kamet, and gave the valley its name. Ninety-five years later it still feels like somewhere you stumble into rather than arrive at.

> The cloud never lifted, so there were no shadows to hide in. Everything was lit the same, and every colour had to earn its place in the frame.

## Small things in the rain

Grey light is unkind to wide views and generous to close ones. With no hard shadows, petals show every fold, and the rain leaves beads on everything.

![Pink balsam flowers with raindrops on their stems among green leaves](valley-of-flowers-2026/pink-balsam-01.avif)

Wild balsam grows in thickets along the path, pink against a hundred shades of green. I got low, waited for the wind to drop, and focused on the drops hanging from the stems rather than on the flowers.

![A person in a checked shirt sits on a rock in a meadow of tall white flowers below misty green hills](valley-of-flowers-2026/flower-meadow-01.avif "wide")

A meadow this big needs a person in it before its size makes sense, so I sat on a rock in the middle of it. The flowers nearest the lens came up to eye level, the hills behind went soft in the mist, and for once nothing in the frame was moving. This is the picture I keep coming back to.

## Cloud over the peaks

On the way out the weather finally moved. Cloud poured over the ridges like something coming to the boil, and for ten minutes the peak above us kept appearing and vanishing.

![Cloud pours over a dark mountain peak under a grey sky](valley-of-flowers-2026/cloud-peak-01.avif "wide")

There is a power line across the corner of this frame. I could have cropped it out. I left it in, as a reminder of how close the village is to all of this.

## If you go

- September is quieter than July and August. There are fewer flowers and paler ones, the paths are emptier, and the cloud does half the composing for you.
- Pack for rain every day. A rain cover for the camera bag and a dry cloth in every pocket matter more than any lens.
- The park is open by day only, so leave Ghangaria early. The best light is the first light that gets through.
- Look down as often as you look up. The wide views get the attention; the small things tell the story.
