Triund is the walk everyone in Dharamshala tells you to do, and for good reason. A few hours of steady climbing from the edge of McLeod Ganj puts you on a grassy ridge at about 2,850 metres, face to face with the Dhauladhar. I went up in June, at the very start of the monsoon, when the weather changes every twenty minutes and the views come and go.

## Before the trail

The walk starts in the market, and so did the photographs.

![Pack mules loaded with gas cylinders stand in a busy market lane in hazy sunlight](triund-2026/market-mules-01.avif "wide")

Almost everything at the top of Triund, from the gas to the rice to the bottled water at the tea stalls, goes up on the backs of mules. I found this pair waiting in a lane beside a cart advertising its famous aloo puri, cylinders strapped on, with the morning sun coming through the dust behind them. I shot into the light on purpose and let the haze soften everything. It is the warmest frame of the trip, and the last one with a clear horizon for a while.

## Into the cloud forest

Above the last houses the path climbs through oak and rhododendron, and in June the cloud lives in the forest.

![Mist drifting over steep mountain slopes covered in dense green forest](triund-2026/cloud-forest-01.avif "wide")

Fog is a gift for photographs and a problem for exposure. The camera sees a bright grey world and tries to turn it into middle grey, so I added about a stop of exposure to keep the cloud white instead of dull.

![A hiker with a backpack stands on a rocky mountain trail, looking up into thick white cloud](triund-2026/trail-01.avif "wide")

Thick cloud simplifies a composition for you: one figure, one rock, and the rest of the frame white. You stop composing with lines and start composing with whatever is left.

## Best View Half Point

Halfway up, a hand-painted sign promises the best view, and a stone wall gives you somewhere to sit and find out. When I got there, the view was entirely cloud. A dog had got there first.

![A shaggy dog sits on a stone wall beside a Best View Half Point sign and prayer flags, cloud filling the valley below](triund-2026/half-point-01.avif "wide")

The dogs of Triund are famous among trekkers: they adopt a group at the bottom and walk with it all the way up. This one sat on the wall staring into the cloud for as long as I stayed, as if waiting for the view to come back. I went wide to keep the sign, the prayer flags and the drop in one frame, then waited for it to turn its head.

> Some days the view is the weather. You photograph what it gives you and let the mountain keep the rest.

## On the ridge

The cloud broke near the top. Below us the Kangra valley stayed buried; above, the snow on the Dhauladhar came out one patch at a time.

![A brown cow walks along a grassy ridge in front of a bank of cloud, snowy peaks rising above it](triund-2026/ridge-cow-01.avif "wide")

The ridge is grazing land as much as a viewpoint. Cows wander along it at their own pace, and shepherds bring their flocks through on the way to higher pastures. I got low so this cow would walk across the bank of cloud rather than the hillside, and pressed the shutter when its legs made a clean shape against the white.

Earlier on the trail, a flock of sheep had pushed past me, and that was the moment I switched from stills to video. A photograph could not hold the way they moved together, shoulder to shoulder, all of them turning at once. The short loop at the top of the [Triund trip page](/travel/himachal-pradesh/triund-2026) comes from that minute.

## Night, and the morning after

![A dark night sky full of stars above a faint glow on the horizon](triund-2026/night-sky-01.avif)

After dark the sky cleared completely. The glow along the bottom of this frame is the light of the towns in the valley, softened by the cloud lying over them. A few seconds of exposure was enough: the stars stay as points, and the haze turns into a soft band of light.

![Snow-streaked rocky peaks rising out of a sea of cloud under a blue sky](triund-2026/dhauladhar-01.avif "wide")

By morning the cloud had settled below the ridge, and the Dhauladhar stood clear above it. The name means "white range", and with the sun on it you can see why.

![A steep granite peak with patches of snow against a clear blue sky](triund-2026/dhauladhar-02.avif)

With a longer lens the range stops being scenery and becomes texture: granite slabs, snow caught in the gullies, and a sky so clean it barely looks real after two days inside a cloud.

## If you go

- Start early. In the monsoon the cloud usually builds by late morning, and the clearest views come at first light.
- Give the mules the path. When a mule train passes, step to the hillside of the trail, never the drop.
- Carry a warm layer even in June. The ridge is cold once the sun goes down.
- Keep a rain cover on the camera bag and a cloth in your pocket. In cloud, lenses fog and bags soak through faster than you expect.
